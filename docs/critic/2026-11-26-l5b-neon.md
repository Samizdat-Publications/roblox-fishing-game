**Score: 5.5/10**

There is a genuinely charming foundation here: the dawn and golden-hour lighting gradients, chunky wooden dock, striped tackle stalls, cattail weedbeds, and distant pine hills already hint at a cozy, readable fishing game. What holds the world back right now is geometry and water presentation - the main lake, Cedar Creek riffle, and pool are built as rigid 90-degree rectangles with bright swimming-pool cyan water and a stark flat wood-plank dividing wall, making the fishing spots feel like municipal retention ponds instead of memorable natural waters.

Top fixes:
1. Break up the rigid 90-degree rectangles across the lake, stream, and paths: reshape the main lake (07), the laser-straight riffle ditch (09), and the boxy pool (10) with curved or staggered low-poly banks, angled boulder clusters, and gentle sandy/muddy shorelines, and replace the giant flat wooden wall at Cedar Creek (11) with a natural treeline, rocky pass, or rustic split-rail fence and wooden arch.
2. Fix the water palette, seams, and night visibility: shift the WaterColor away from bright swimming-pool cyan (03, 08) toward a deeper, slightly greener natural lake tone with stronger sky reflectance, remove the visible straight seam cutting across the pool water (10, 12), tone down how dock and shore lights turn shallow water neon-teal at night (04, 07, 12), and add a subtle emissive tip or ripple glow to the bobber at night (04) so bites remain readable on phones.
3. Give midday lighting and shorelines more life: in noon shots (03, 08, 11), add soft Atmosphere haze, a warmer Sun tint, and chunky low-poly clouds so the sky is not flat blue, and frame the far and side banks with varied clusters of pines, broadleaf trees, shrubs, and rocks instead of an even row of identical cattail clumps along a straight path.
