Date: 2026-11-25 23:21. Rubric: `sound`.

**Score: 6/10**

A genuinely cozy, promising first sound pass with a strong instinct for mood - the dawn birdsong, calm acoustic guitar fading out on cast, and weight-scaled catch splashes already establish the calm-but-tense identity. Right now the illusion is held back by a massively oversized cast splash, identical unvaried fight/wait loops, a delayed discovery chime, and bite sounds that either feel too quiet (tap) or too sub-bass-heavy for phone speakers (deep pull). Note: the clip contains two completed catches (Perch at 00:11, Carp at 00:25) and cuts off right after the third cast at 00:28.

Top fixes:
1. Replace the oversized cast splash (00:03, 00:14.8, 00:28.5) with a shorter, lighter bobber plop about 6-8 dB quieter, and add a brief 0.2s soft rod/line whoosh right before it so casting stops shattering the dawn calm.
2. Punch up bites for small phone speakers and differentiate fights: add a crisper mid-range water snap/rod-tug to both the quiet perch tap (00:08.5) and the sub-heavy carp rumble (00:21), and layer a subtle line-strain/drag sound plus small thrash splashes during heavier fights (00:22-00:24.5) so a 2 kg carp does not sound identical to a 0.13 kg perch.
3. Tighten catch reveal timing and add loop variation: trigger the new-species discovery chime ~1 second earlier (around 00:25.5 right off the splash tail instead of 00:26.5), raise the Common catch stinger at 00:11 by ~3 dB so it does not get lost under the splash, and add subtle pitch/timing variation (+/-5-8%) to the bobber plops (00:05, 00:07, 00:17, 00:19) so long waits never sound mechanical.

Strengths:
- Dawn ambience (00:00-00:03, 00:12-00:14) feels peaceful and natural, and ducking the calm acoustic guitar while a line is in the water immediately creates a quiet, focused atmosphere.
- Catch splash weight scaling works well: the light perch splash at 00:11 contrasts clearly with the heavy, satisfying 1.98 kg carp splash at 00:25.
- Mechanical reel clicks (00:09-00:11, 00:22-00:24) sound clean, tactile, and closely synced to holding the reel input.
- Differentiating bite styles by fish behavior is a great design call - the deep underwater rumble on the carp at 00:21 feels unmistakably heavier than the perch tap at 00:08.
- The discovery chime for the first-ever Common Carp (00:26) has a bright, celebratory character that stands apart from the understated regular catch.

Broken or ugly:
- Cast splash at 00:03, 00:14.8, and 00:28.5 is far too heavy and loud for a bobber, sounding like a large rock or body hitting the lake, with no preceding cast/line-whoosh sound.
- New-species discovery chime for the Common Carp is out of sync by roughly 1.2-1.5 seconds: the catch splash and card appear at 00:25, followed by a dead pause before the chime plays at 00:26.5.
- Acoustic guitar theme cuts out abruptly on cast at 00:14.5 instead of fading smoothly over ~0.5-1 second.
- No subtle lapping water bed beneath the birdsong, leaving near-silence between chirps, and no UI sound when bait auto-switches to Minnows at 00:28.

Action taken: the heavy built-in cast splash is gone (a light, pitched-up plop instead); bites play louder with a crisp water snap on top; one-shots vary in pitch a few percent; the discovery chime moved from 1.6 s to 0.9 s after the catch; the theme now waits for 10 s with no line in, so it can't start and then cut on the next cast. Not done: a cast whoosh (the existing cast swoosh stays) and a bait-switch UI sound. Re-reviewed: 7/10.
