# Rarity and Godlike fish: Stewart's direction (2026-11-26)

This adds to `DIRECTION.md` section 3 and answers the "Godlike fish" and "should Legendaries pay less" questions in `OVERNIGHT-SUMMARY-2026-11-26.md`.

## How often each tier shows up

Balance it like a normal well-tuned collecting game. The rarer the tier, the rarer the catch, but every tier is reachable.

- **Legendary: semi-often, and a big deal every time.** A player who fishes the right spot, time and bait should land one about once every session or two, never every few minutes. When it hooks, the game should stop and make a moment of it: a heavier pre-bite cue, a harder fight, the full stinger, a banner and a journal entry.
- **Mythic: rarer, and tied to conditions.** Weather, moon or place, so players plan for them.
- **Godlike: the rarest thing in the game.** Most players hear about one before they ever see one.

Starting targets per catch, for a player fishing a spot well (tune with `tools/econ/sim.py` and playtests; these are a shape, not law):

| Tier | Share of catches | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| Common | about 55% | every cast |
| Uncommon | about 25% | most sessions, several times |
| Rare | about 12% | a few per session |
| Epic | about 5% | once or twice a session |
| Legendary | about 2 to 3% | about once every session or two |
| Mythic | well under 1%, only in its conditions | a planned hunt |
| Godlike | a tiny fraction, only during its event | a server legend |

## Value matches rarity

A fish is worth about what its rarity says it should be. A Legendary is not cheaper than its rarity, and not a money printer either. Rule of thumb: a fish of a tier pays roughly what a player would have earned fishing normally in the time it usually takes to find one, plus a premium for the moment. So hunting rare fish pays fairly, the shop curve stays intact, and the real reward is the story, the journal and the trophy case. Re-run the economy check after setting prices. This replaces the idea of Legendaries paying less.

## Godlike fish: giant myth-level monsters

Godlike fish are not just big fish. They are legendary creatures from lake and sea myths: a colossal squid, an ancient tentacled thing from the deep, a giant armoured lake beast that rises out of a storm. The inspiration is the classic sea monster, the kraken and the kaiju, but **every design, name and look is our own**. No borrowed names or characters: not Godzilla, not Cthulhu, nothing that copies a film, a book or another game.

Ideas to develop (with Liam, it's a great job for him):
- **A colossal squid in the Big Lake's blue water,** only on a stormy night. It takes the two-person Deep-V: one steers to keep the line from snapping, one fights.
- **An ancient tentacled one in the deepest hole,** under a full moon. The water goes still and the lights flicker before it bites.
- **An armoured lake beast that wakes in a thunderstorm.** The whole server feels it: the ground shakes, birds scatter, and a roar rolls across the water.

How they work:
- **An event, not a random roll.** Special conditions (place, weather, moon, time) open a short window. They may need a special bait or ritual found through journal rumours.
- **Boss-style fights,** in several phases, harder than anything else, and best with a friend.
- **A server-wide moment:** an announcement, music, and the creature visible above the water.
- **Kept forever:** a catch is one per player (or one per crew), goes in the trophy case, and is told in the journal. Size and value are the biggest in the game.
- **Spooky, never gory.** It must stay within the Minimal or Mild rating for kids: awe and a shiver, no blood or horror imagery.

Start with one Godlike creature and make it unforgettable before adding a second.
